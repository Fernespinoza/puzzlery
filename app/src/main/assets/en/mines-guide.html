Strategy guide

First moves

Your first tap is always safe; work from the edge of whatever it opens. Hunt for numbers touching only one hidden cell — that cell is a mine, so flag it. Each flag may complete the count for the number beside it, which makes that number's other cells safe to open. Work the boundary in that chain: flag, open the cells that become safe, repeat. The corners of a cleared area usually resolve first, because the numbers there touch fewer hidden cells.

Key patterns

Learn the straight-edge runs. Along a flat boundary, 1-2-1 means the cells in front of the two 1s are mines and the cell in front of the 2 is safe. A 3 sitting in a board corner touches only three cells, so all three are mines — and that usually settles the 2 beside it. Where two numbers share most of their hidden cells, subtract: if every hidden cell a 1 can see is also seen by a 2 next to it, the 2's second mine hides in the cells only the 2 can see. Keep a running count of flags against the board's total; late on, it solves positions that look like coin flips.

Common traps

Flagging hunches. A wrong flag is worse than no flag: it satisfies numbers falsely and detonates a mine two moves later, somewhere that feels unrelated. Never open a cell next to a number before checking whether flags already placed complete its count. In this Mines a guess is never truly forced — the boards are built to fall to logic alone, so if two options look equal, you have missed a deduction: re-check the numbers whose flags almost complete them, and count your flags against the board's total before touching anything dark.

Sources

  • "Your first tap is always safe" — in this app it is stronger than safe: the engine arranges for the first opened square to be a 0, and a built-in solver verifies every generated board. Puzzlery's own engine source, app/src/main/jni/mines.c ("Minesweeper solver, used to ensure the generated grids are solvable without having to take risks"; the first square is 0 "by deliberate arrangement"). The same guarantee stated publicly for Tatham's Mines — first probe always safe, no map asks you to guess: "Minesweeper online: no guessing, ever", https://www.neurodual.fr/en/games/minesweeper
  • The working rules the guide chains through — if a number's flagged neighbours equal the number, its other neighbours are safe; if flags plus covered squares equal the number, every covered neighbour is a mine (this is what makes a corner 3 an all-mines cell); comparing two numbers that share covered squares is subtraction: "Minesweeper: rules, patterns and a free board to play", https://arkedia.judeen.dev/games/minesweeper/
  • The 1-2-1 pattern, mines under the two 1s and the cell in front of the 2 safe: same two sources above.
