Strategy guide

First moves

Start at the corners and edges, where the numbers have fewer ways to work. In a corner, a 3 always takes both edges along the border, and a 1 never takes either of them. A 0 anywhere kills all four edges around its cell — mark those crosses first. A 3 sitting next to a 0 is fully solved: the edge they share is empty, and its other three edges are all lines.

Key patterns

Think at the corners of cells, not in the cells. Every point where grid lines meet ends with zero lines or exactly two. So if two lines meet at a point, the other edges there are empty; if one line arrives and two edges there are crossed out, the last edge is forced. Follow those forced turns and the loop draws itself in stretches. A 3 diagonally next to a 0 takes the two edges at the corner it shares with the 0. On big boards, shade regions inside or outside the loop as lines separate them; an empty edge merges two regions into the same side, and contradictions in the shading expose mistakes early.

Common traps

Closing a loop that is not the whole loop. The answer is a single circuit, so any small ring completed early is wrong, however tidy it looks — leave one edge open until the end. Drawing a third line into a point is the other classic dead end; check the far end of every segment before you place it. Do not skip the 0s because they look like nothing; they are the only free information on the board. These are square-grid patterns; on the triangular and hexagonal boards the corner counts change, but the points rule — zero or two — does not.

Sources

  • The 0 (all four edges crossed), the corner rules (a corner 1 uses neither border edge; a corner 3 uses both), the 3 next to a 0 (shared edge empty, the 3's other three edges are lines), and the points rule (every vertex ends with 0 or exactly 2 lines): "Slitherlink Rules: How to Play the Loop Puzzle", https://www.thepuzzlelabs.com/slitherlink/rules — and "Slitherlink — ToolsFlare", https://toolsflare.com/slitherlink?p=ka1eney
  • Inside/outside region shading is bookkeeping on the same fact those sources state as rule one: all lines form a single closed loop, so an empty edge merges two areas onto the same side of it. Premature small loops are excluded by the same single-loop rule.
  • Correction applied 2026-11-14 on Fernando's approval: the Key patterns sentence originally said a 3 diagonally next to a 0 "takes the two edges at its far corner" — wrong. The standard result is the opposite corner: both sides of the 3 that meet the 0's corner (the corner the two cells share) are lines — "If a 3 is adjacent to a 0 diagonally, both sides of the 3 that meet the 0's corner must be filled": Slitherlink solving guide (Wikibooks "Puzzles/Stripe", mirrored at https://En-Academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/1241663). Short proof: at the shared corner, the 0's two edges are crossed, so the 3's two edges there are both-or-neither under the points rule — and a 3 cannot afford both empty. The sentence now reads "at the corner it shares with the 0".
